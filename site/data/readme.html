Ali ibn Suleiman, Commentary on Genesis

English translation of the Genesis portion of ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān al-Muqaddasī's Pentateuch commentary, edited by Solomon L. Skoss (Philadelphia, 1928).

ʿAlī ibn Sulaymān (Hebrew: Eli ben Shelomo) was one of the last great Karaite scholars of medieval Jerusalem. He flourished in the closing decades of the eleventh century and continued working into the early twelfth; a note in his own hand is dated to 1103 CE. Sometime before the First Crusade reached Jerusalem in 1099 he left the city — almost certainly for Fustat (Old Cairo) — and he was among the last of the avelei Tziyon, the Karaite community that had made Jerusalem the spiritual and intellectual center of Karaite Judaism for two centuries.

The commentary translated here is Ali's intizāʿ (digest) of Abū ʾl-Faraj Hārūn's abridgement of Joseph ibn Nūḥ's Pentateuch commentary — three layers of a Karaite Bible-reading tradition that did not survive the Crusader sack. The Genesis portion was edited and published by Solomon L. Skoss in 1928; Skoss's text is the base for this translation.
