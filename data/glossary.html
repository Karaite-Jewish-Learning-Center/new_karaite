Glossary

Recurring technical Arabic vocabulary in Ali's footnotes. Compiled from 615 footnotes across all 28 chapters.

TermArabicDefinition
A. Kalām & theology
al-taklīfالتكليف"[legal] obligation" The kalām category of acts that fall under divine command rather than mere permission. The cluster of doctrines around taklīf is the central Muʿtazilite-Karaite ethical framework Ali presupposes throughout.
taklīf lā yuṭāqتكليف لا يطاق"an obligation that cannot be borne"; the classical category of "imposition beyond capacity," rejected as theologically impossible by the Muʿtazila (and so by Ali).
al-mukallaf / al-mukallafīnالمكلَّف / المكلَّفين"The [morally] obligated, the one legally charged, the [responsible] agent." The standard Muʿtazilite designation for the class of rational creatures upon whom religious obligation falls.
qādir and mutamakkinقادر and متمكِّنA paired kalām formula for the preconditions of moral accountability: qādir is "capable" (granted the ability to act), mutamakkin is "enabled, in firm possession of the power to act" (unobstructed in the exercise of that ability). Together they constitute the kalām conditions that must be satisfied before taklīf can rightly attach to an agent.
al-ʿādaالعادة"[established] custom / regular course of nature" The kalām term for the ordinary order of nature as God's habitual mode of acting (al-ʿādāt al-ilāhiyya). For Ash'arites this language replaces nature-causality outright; for the Muʿtazilī-leaning Karaites the picture is softer, but the regularity is still a divine habit, not an intrinsic necessity.
naqḍ ʿādaنقض عادة"breach of custom"; the standard kalām/uṣūl designation of the miraculous as a punctuated interruption of the regularity God customarily maintains.
al-ṣalāḥالصلاح"right action, [moral] soundness" In the Muʿtazilite kalām that informs much Karaite theology, the ethically right course of action. Distinguished from al-aṣlaḥ (الأصلح), "the [ethically] optimal" — the doctrine that God must will the most salutary outcome for His creatures.
al-taʿlīlالتعليل"providing the rationale, [stating the] efficient cause" The uṣūl al-fiqh term for indicating the legal reason behind a ruling. Karaite hermeneutics widely admits taʿlīl of biblical commandments where the text yields a discernible rationale.
al-shubhaالشبهة"[evidential] doubt, [the appearance of warrant]" The uṣūl al-fiqh category of the apparent-but-unsound reason that mitigates culpability. An agent acting under genuine shubha is not held fully responsible for what would otherwise be a wrong.
al-tawfīqالتوفيق"[divine] success-granting, [the] gracious-aid" The kalām term for the divine assistance that conduces to the agent's right action — contrasted with khidhlān (خذلان, "abandonment"), God's withholding of that aid.
ʿilm / ʿulūmعلم / علوم"knowledge" / "[branches of] knowledge" Propositional knowledge; in some contexts the cognitive faculties God implants. Often paired or contrasted with qudar (قُدَر), the [granted] capacities for action — the standard kalām pair: God endows each mukallaf with the ʿulūm and qudar required to discharge what He commands.
manzilaمنزلة"rank, station" Technical kalām term for the moral-ontological status of an agent. The Muʿtazilite slogan al-manzila bayna al-manzilatayn ("the position between the two positions") refers to their intermediate status of the grave sinner — neither believer nor unbeliever. Ali uses manzila generally for moral standing.
qabīḥقبيح"[ethically] ugly, [morally] foul" The Muʿtazilite-Karaite category of objective moral wrongness — paired with ḥasan ("[ethically] good"). Acts are qabīḥ in themselves, not merely because God has forbidden them; this is the cornerstone of the Muʿtazilite-Karaite rejection of divine-command voluntarism.
al-rasūlالرسول"the messenger" Ali's regular designation for Moses, usually with the honorific ʿalayhi al-salām "peace be upon him."
B. Hermeneutics & epistemology
ḥaqīqa and majāzحقيقة and مجاز"literal / figurative sense" The fundamental Arabic-rhetoric/uṣūl pair: ḥaqīqa is the primary lexical sense of an expression; majāz the metaphorical or extended sense. A central Karaite hermeneutic distinction, developed especially by Abū l-Faraj Hārūn.
al-ẓāhirالظاهر"the plain [sense], the manifest reading" The surface meaning of the text. Standardly contrasts with al-bāṭin ("the hidden, interior") and with majāz.
taʾwīlتأويل"interpretation, esoteric reading" The principled departure from al-ẓāhir when reason or another verse forbids the literal reading. Karaite taʾwīl is reason-constrained: it is admissible only where it is required, never as a default-mode of reading.
samʿ / ʿaqlسمع / عقل"revealed report / reason" The Karaite-Muʿtazilite epistemological distinction between truths accessible to the unaided intellect (ʿaqlan) and those given only by authoritative transmission (samʿan). Many religious obligations (e.g., specific ritual forms) are samʿī; the existence of God and the wrongness of injustice are ʿaqlī.
sharʿīشرعي"legal, [pertaining to] revealed Law"; the technical pair lughawī / sharʿī (lexical / legal) is a standard Karaite analytic distinction for whether a term's reference is fixed by language or by revelation.
al-aqrabالأقرب"the most plausible [reading]" Ali's signature marker for his own preferred reading after surveying alternatives. Contrasts with baʿīd (بعيد) — "improbable, far-fetched."
qiyāsقياس"analogy" The rule of regular morphological/conceptual parallelism — central Karaite hermeneutic-grammatical category. Where attested, an analogical inference is guaranteed by the regular pattern, even where not directly attested (adverb: qiyāsan قياساً, "by analogy").
dalālaدلالة"[conventional] indication" The kalām/uṣūl term for the relation by which an expression indicates its meaning. Ali invokes dalāla in arguments that God cannot speak ambiguously where comprehension is required: the very purpose of revealed address presupposes a stable dalāla — and so [[taʾkhīr-al-bayān]] is ruled out in the strong form.
taʾkhīr al-bayānتأخير البيان"delaying of clarification" The uṣūl al-fiqh problem of whether a divine pronouncement may issue ambiguously and only later be clarified. The Karaites and the Muʿtazila reject this in the strong form: any ambiguity at the time-of-need would be a defect in divine speech.
istithnāʾاستثناء"[an] unstated condition, [implicit] exception" The uṣūl al-fiqh term for an unspoken qualifying clause that, if operative, would restrict an otherwise unconditional pronouncement. Ali uses istithnāʾ to argue that some apparent divine breaches of promise are actually the operation of an implicit conditional.
mafsadaمفسدة"corruption, [thing that] produces harm" The legal-theoretical category of an act that yields net evil. Paired conceptually with maṣlaḥa (مصلحة) — the act that yields net benefit.
amāraأمارة"sign, indicator" The diagnostic feature by which one identifies a particular semantic value of a polysemous form. Used by Ali both for textual signs (an amāra in the verse pointing to one reading over another) and for evidential signs in argument.
fī al-taḥqīqفي التحقيق"in actual fact" Pairs with al-ẓāhir as the level of underlying truth opposed to the surface impression. Where the ẓāhir of a verse says X but fī al-taḥqīq the matter is Y, Ali is signaling that the apparent reading is misleading.
maʿrifaمعرفة"[discursive] understanding" In kalām: the cognitive grasp of a particular, often distinguished from ʿilm (propositional knowledge). Watch the polysemy: in grammatical contexts (e.g., Gen 9:228) maʿrifa means "[grammatically] definite" — a noun made determinate by the article, construct, or pronominal suffix, as opposed to nakira (نكرة, "indefinite").
masʾalaمسألة"question, dialectical problem" The Karaite dialectical-question marker that opens a hypothetical objection answered below. Closely linked to the in qīla / kāna al-jawāb formula (see Dialectic).
C. Grammar (naḥw)
fiʿl mā lam yusamma fāʿiluhuفعل ما لم يسمَّ فاعله"A verb whose agent is not named" — the standard Arabic-grammar term for the morphological passive (e.g., ḍuriba). Karaite grammarians use this designation for the Hebrew niphal / puʿal / hophʿal forms.
wāw al-ʿaṭfواو العطف"the vāv of conjunction" The standard Arabic-grammar designation for the coordinating vāv, taken over for Hebrew morphology. To be distinguished sharply from the nāqila (see below).
nāqilaناقلة"transferring, [tense-]shifting [vāv]" The Karaite grammatical doctrine of the vāv-conversive that toggles the apparent tense of a Hebrew verb. See al-Kāfī §§ on this device. The vāv is one of Ali's standing concerns; he is careful to distinguish wāw al-ʿaṭf from nāqila even when the surface form is identical.
ẓarfظرف"temporal/locative adverb" The Arabic grammatical category of the temporal complement — equivalent to Hebrew mafʿūl fīhi in Abū l-Faraj's typology.
waṣfوصف"descriptive attribute / qualifier" The Arabic-grammar term for a qualifying expression. In analyzing an iḍāfa (genitive construction), interpreting the dependent term as a waṣf means it describes the head's own quality rather than relating it to a distinct entity.
jinsجنس"genus, generic kind" Pairs with nawʿ (نوع), "species, particular kind." A foundational distinction for class-membership arguments.
al-ḍamīrالضمير"the pronoun" The Karaite-naḥw term covering both independent and bound pronouns — including bound personal-elements of verb prefixes and pronominal suffixes whose antecedent must be identified.
amrأمر"imperative" The standard Arabic-grammar term, applied here to Hebrew morphology.
ḥukmحكم"rule, regulation" The regular syntactic/semantic behavior of a lexeme. Watch the polysemy: ḥukm also means "ruling, judgment" (as in Gen 9:228 where Hebrew ba-adam is read as a substantive denoting human judicial agency).
taqdīrتقدير"supplied/virtual reading" The standard Arabic-grammar term for grammatically-required elements that must be understood without being expressed. Adverb: taqdīran (تقديراً), "by way of supplied/virtual reading" — as a notional supplement rather than as an actually-asserted reality. (Ali also uses taqdīr in its lexical sense "proportioning, measuring out," especially when recovering the etymological sense of khalq "creation" from classical Arab lexicography.)
qiblaقبلة"acceptance, [direction-of-prayer]" The Qurʾānic/kalām term for the place to which acceptable prayer is directed. Ali uses qibla in this hierocentric sense in his treatment of Abraham's altar (Gen 22) and Jacob's stone (Gen 28).
maḥaṭṭمحطّ"central [clause], [pivot] point" The rhetorical/syntactic term for the load-bearing element of a multi- clause expression — the clause on which the meaning ultimately settles.
D. Dialectical signature formulae
qīla / wa-qīlaقيل / وقيل"it is said / and it has been said" Introduces an attributed prior view (often one Ali will reject). Cf. CONVENTIONS § Karaite signature formulae.
in qīla … kāna al-jawābإن قيل … كان الجواب"if it be said … the answer is" The two halves of the Karaite masʾala (problem-and-answer) dialectic: a hypothetical objection followed by Ali's reply. The masʾala dialectic is the unit of argument throughout Ali's commentary.
yajrī majrāيجري مجرى"runs the course of, functions as" Ali's signature subsumption-formula bringing a Hebrew construction under a categorial rubric. Inflected forms: yajrī majrāhumā "runs the course of the two of them" (used for paired constructions).
